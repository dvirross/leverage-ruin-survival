## 01-kobe-japan

- **Caption:** The Japanese stock market, in US dollars, around the Kobe earthquake of 17 January 1995 and the collapse of Barings on Sunday 26 February: a fall of 11.8% from the close before the earthquake to the low of 27 February, and a full recovery by 10 April.
- **Alt text:** Line chart of a Japanese stock-market index in US dollars from December 1994 to December 1995, set to 100 at the close of 16 January 1995. It drops 1.3% on the earthquake day, falls to a low of 88.2 (−11.8%) on 27 February, the first trading day after Barings Bank collapsed, and is back at 100 on 10 April; it ends the year at 99.0.
- **Source line:** Kenneth R. French Data Library, Japan daily market return from 1990 (US dollars, value-weighted, including dividends); not the Nikkei 225.
- **Image size:** 2200 x 1000 (suggested `--pf-ratio:2200 / 1000`)

## 02-doubling-game

- **Caption:** A simulated doubling game on a fair coin: the average bankroll stays near 100 whatever the number of bets, but after 5,000 bets 93% of players are ruined and only 6% are ahead; with roulette odds the ruined share reaches 98%.
- **Alt text:** Two stacked charts against the number of bets (10 to 5,000, logarithmic axis). Top: for a fair coin the share of players ahead falls from 82% at 10 bets to 6% at 5,000, while the share ruined rises from 0% to 93%; a dashed line for roulette odds ends at 98% ruined. Bottom: the average bankroll stays between 95 and 100, close to the starting 100.
- **Source line:** Simulation (20,000 simulated players per point, fixed seed); code in the companion repository.
- **Image size:** 2200 x 1200 (suggested `--pf-ratio:2200 / 1200`)

## 03-down-streaks

## 04-works-vs-survives

- **Caption:** What leverage does over 10 years of US stock-market history: the mean return keeps rising, the median growth peaks near 3× and falls beyond, and the chance of a 50% fall climbs from 8% at 1× to 45% at 3×, with wipe-outs appearing at 6×. The Kelly leverage computed from the full sample is 2.68.
- **Alt text:** Two stacked line charts against leverage from 0.5× to 6×, for 10-year windows of daily-rebalanced US market returns since 1926. Top: the mean annual return rises from 10% to 954% at 6× (off the scale above 3×), while the median annualized growth rises to 19% at 3×, then falls to −6.3% at 6×. Bottom: the chance of a 50% fall within the window rises from 2% at 0.5× to 79% at 6×, and the chance of wipe-out is zero until 5× and 11% at 6×. Vertical lines mark the Kelly leverage 2.68 and the grid optimum 2.65.
- **Source line:** Kenneth R. French Data Library, daily market returns 1926–2026 (value-weighted US market including dividends). 10-year windows start every 21st trading day and overlap heavily; financing at the risk-free rate, no costs.
- **Image size:** 2200 x 1400 (suggested `--pf-ratio:2200 / 1400`)

## 05-heatmap-fall

- **Caption:** How often a leveraged position falls by half, and how often a loan is called, by leverage and horizon. At 3× daily-rebalanced, 45% of 10-year windows contain a fall of 50% or more; a fixed 2× loan with 25% maintenance is called in 29% of 10-year windows.
- **Alt text:** Two heatmaps of percentages. Left: probability of a fall of 50% or more at some close, for daily-rebalanced leverage 0.5× to 6× (rows) and horizons of 1, 5, 10 and 20 years (columns); it grows with both, from 0% at 0.5× over 1 year to 87% at 6× over 20 years, and is 45% at 3× over 10 years. Right: probability of a margin call for a fixed loan with 25% maintenance, leverage 1.25× to 3×; at 2× it is 6% over 1 year and 29% over 10 years, and at 3× 57% over 10 years.
- **Source line:** Kenneth R. French Data Library, daily market returns 1926–2026 (value-weighted US market including dividends). Windows start every 21st trading day and overlap; daily closes only, so intraday gaps are not modelled.
- **Image size:** 2200 x 1000 (suggested `--pf-ratio:2200 / 1000`)

## 06-crash-paths

- **Caption:** Four real episodes, each starting about a month before the crash. At 3× the 1929–1932 episode shrinks 100 to 0.1 and the 2007–2009 episode to 6; a fixed 2× loan was called in 1929–1932 and 2007–2009 and survived 1987 and 2020.
- **Alt text:** Four small charts on a logarithmic vertical axis, each starting at 100 about a month before a crash: 1929–1932, 1987, 2007–2009 and 2020. Each shows the market at 1×, and at 2× and 3× with daily rebalancing, and a 2× fixed loan with 25% maintenance margin. Lowest values at 1× / 2× / 3×: 1929–1932 17 / 2 / 0.1; 1987 71 / 46 / 26.7; 2007–2009 49 / 19 / 6.1; 2020 67 / 42 / 24.4. The loan was called, and the position closed, on 29 Oct 1929 (1929–1932, equity 33); 9 Oct 2008 (2007–2009, equity 27); it was never called in 1987 or 2020.
- **Source line:** Kenneth R. French Data Library, daily market returns 1926–2026 (value-weighted US market including dividends). Daily closes; the loan position closes at the first close below 25% equity, and equity then earns the T-bill rate.
- **Image size:** 2200 x 1400 (suggested `--pf-ratio:2200 / 1400`)

## 07-fat-tails

- **Caption:** Days on which the US market fell by more than k standard deviations, against the count expected under a normal model with the same mean and spread. Beyond 5 standard deviations there were 51 such days against 0.008 expected; the 19 October 1987 fall (−17.4%) was 16 standard deviations.
- **Alt text:** Chart on a logarithmic vertical axis of the number of down days beyond k standard deviations, k = 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10. Observed: 234, 105, 51, 29, 9, 4. Normal-model expectation: 36, 0.83, 0.0075, 2.6e-05, 1.6e-11, 2e-19, labelled as one such day in 2.9 years, 125 years, 10^4 years, 10^7 years, 10^13 years, 10^21 years. A separate point marks 19 October 1987, 16.2 standard deviations below the mean, which the normal model would expect once in about 10^56 years.
- **Source line:** Kenneth R. French Data Library, daily market returns 1926–2026 (value-weighted US market including dividends). Standard deviation and mean are those of the full sample; down days only.

## 08-early-short

- **Caption:** The US stock market relative to 5 December 1996, the day of Greenspan's “irrational exuberance” speech. A short position opened that day was called on 18 Mar 1999 (index up 73%), the market peaked 120% above entry on 24 Mar 2000, and even the October 2002 low was 12% above it.
- **Alt text:** Line chart of the US total-return index from 5 December 1996 (= 1) to the end of 2004. It rises above 1 from early 1997, reaches 2.20 on 24 Mar 2000 and falls to 1.12 on 9 Oct 2002, never back to 1; the area between the line and 1 is shaded. Marked: the margin call of a 1× short on 18 Mar 1999 when the index stood at 1.73, the peak, and the 2002 low.
- **Source line:** Kenneth R. French Data Library, daily market returns 1926–2026 (value-weighted US market including dividends). Index built from daily returns including dividends (a short pays them); margin call at 30% maintenance, checked at closes.
- **Image size:** 2200 x 1000 (suggested `--pf-ratio:2200 / 1000`)
